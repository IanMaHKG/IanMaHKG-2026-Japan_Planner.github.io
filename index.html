Practical Tips

Everything you need to prepare before departure.

🚆

Transport Passes

  • Suica / PASMO card — Tap-and-go for all trains, buses, and convenience stores in Tokyo. Add to Apple/Google Wallet.
  • JR Kansai Area Pass (4-day) — Covers JR trains in Kyoto, Osaka, Nara, Kobe. Buy online before departure for discount.
  • Rental car — Book via Nissan Rent-a-Car or Toyota Rent-a-Car. Request studless winter tires + GPS with English. One-way drop-off fee applies (Tokyo → Nagoya ≈ ¥15,000–20,000).
  • International Driving Permit (IDP) — Obtain this in your home country before the trip. Required for all foreign drivers in Japan.
🎫

Advance Bookings

  • Ryokan (Mt Fuji / Hakone) — Book 2–3 months ahead. Request "Fuji-view" room specifically.
  • teamLab — Online tickets only, sell out fast. Book 2+ weeks ahead.
  • Restaurants — Reserve popular spots 3+ weeks ahead for Christmas week. Use Tabelog or ask your hotel concierge.
  • Fushimi Inari — Free entry, no reservation needed, but go early (before 9 AM) to beat crowds.
🙏

Etiquette & Culture

  • Shoes off — Remove shoes when entering homes, ryokans, some restaurants, and temple halls.
  • Quiet trains — Keep phone on silent; avoid calls or loud conversations on trains.
  • Onsen rules — Wash thoroughly before entering. Tattoos may need cover-up patches (ask beforehand). Towels stay out of the water.
  • Cash — Carry ¥10,000–20,000 in cash. Many small restaurants and temples are cash-only. 7-Eleven ATMs accept foreign cards.
  • Tipping — Never tip in Japan. It can be considered rude.
❄️

Winter Driving Safety

  • Drive in daylight only — Sunset is ~16:30 in December. Plan arrivals before dark.
  • Check weather daily — If heavy snow is forecast, skip the drive and take a train instead.
  • Go slow on mountain roads — Extra caution on bridges, tunnels, and shaded curves where black ice forms.
  • Konbini pit stops — 7-Eleven, Lawson, FamilyMart are everywhere. Great for snacks, bathrooms, and hot drinks.
  • Keep headlights on — Even during daytime driving for visibility.

01 Dec 20

Arrival in Tokyo — Asakusa & Skytree

🏙️ Tokyo 🧘 Relaxed
▼
🌅 Morning

Arrive at Narita / Haneda Airport

Clear customs, pick up your Suica card at the airport station, and take the Narita Express or Limousine Bus to your hotel. Check in and rest after the flight.

🍳 Breakfast / Brunch: Grab a quick meal at a konbini (7-Eleven onigiri, sandwiches, and hot coffee) — surprisingly delicious and perfect after a long flight.
🌤️ Afternoon

Senso-ji Temple & Nakamise-dori

Tokyo's oldest temple. Walk through the iconic Kaminarimon (Thunder Gate) and browse the traditional shopping street. Pick up souvenirs, taste ningyo-yaki (sweet bean cakes) and age-manju (fried pastries).

🍜 Lunch: Sometaro (Asakusa) — A charming, family-friendly spot where you cook your own okonomiyaki on a tabletop griddle. Fun and delicious.
🌆 Evening

Tokyo Skytree & Solamachi

Head to Tokyo Skytree (634m) for sunset and city lights views. The Tembo Deck offers a 360° panorama. Browse Solamachi shopping complex below.

🍱 Dinner: Asakusa Imahan — A legendary sukiyaki restaurant (est. 1895). The A5 wagyu sukiyaki set is an unforgettable first-night-in-Japan dinner. Reserve ahead.
💡 Tip: If arriving early, consider visiting Ueno Park (10 min from Asakusa) for a peaceful stroll by Shinobazu Pond. The Ameyoko market nearby is great for snacks.
02 Dec 21

Tokyo Highlights — Shibuya, Harajuku & Illuminations

🏙️ Tokyo ⚖️ Balanced
▼
🌅 Morning

Meiji Shrine & Harajuku

Start with a serene walk through the forested approach to Meiji Shrine — one of Tokyo's most important Shinto shrines. Then stroll Takeshita Street for Harajuku's famous youth culture and unique shops.

🍳 Breakfast: Bills Omotesando — Famous for "the world's best scrambled eggs" and fluffy ricotta pancakes. A relaxed, Western-style brunch your parents will love.
🌤️ Afternoon

Shibuya Crossing & Shibuya Sky

Experience the world's busiest pedestrian crossing from above at Shibuya Sky (230m rooftop observation). Then explore the vibrant streets below. Visit the Hachiko statue for the classic photo op.

🍜 Lunch: Gyukatsu Motomura (Shibuya) — Deep-fried wagyu cutlet served with a hot stone to sear it to your liking. An absolute must-try.
🌆 Evening

Omotesando Illuminations & Roppongi

Walk along Omotesando boulevard to see the elegant champagne-gold winter illuminations draped across the zelkova trees. Then head to Roppongi Hills Keyakizaka for the stunning blue-and-white light tunnel.

🍱 Dinner: Gonpachi Nishi-Azabu (the "Kill Bill" restaurant) — Beautiful traditional interiors, great soba noodles and yakitori. Fun atmosphere and foreigner-friendly.
💡 Tip: Book Shibuya Sky tickets online 1–2 weeks ahead to skip the queue. Sunset time slot (~16:00) is best for both daylight and night views.
03 Dec 22

Drive to Kawaguchiko — Mt Fuji Views

🏔️ Mt Fuji 🚗 Driving
▼
🌅 Morning

Pick Up Rental Car & Depart Tokyo

Pick up your rental car at Shinjuku (Toyota / Nissan Rent-a-Car). Confirm studless winter tires and English GPS. Drive west on the Chuo Expressway towards Kawaguchiko (~2 hours). Depart by 8:00 AM to avoid traffic and arrive in daylight.

🍳 Breakfast: Shinjuku station food hall — Quick and delicious. Try fresh pastries at Boul'Ange or a teishoku (set meal) at any station restaurant.
🌤️ Afternoon

Chureito Pagoda & Lake Kawaguchi

Visit the iconic Chureito Pagoda — 398 steps up, but the view of Mt Fuji behind the red pagoda is priceless. Then drive to the north shore of Lake Kawaguchi for mirror-like reflections of Fuji (best before wind picks up). Stop at Oishi Park for a lakeside stroll.

🍜 Lunch: Hoto Fudo (Kawaguchiko) — Famous for hoto, a hearty flat-noodle miso soup with seasonal vegetables. The ultimate winter comfort food. The cave-like restaurant branch is unique.
🌆 Evening

Check In to Ryokan & Onsen

Check into your ryokan by Lake Kawaguchi. Enjoy the traditional multi-course kaiseki dinner served in your room, followed by a soak in the hot spring baths with views of the snow-capped mountain.

🍱 Dinner: Kaiseki at your ryokan — Included with your stay. Expect 8–12 courses featuring seasonal ingredients, local freshwater fish, and beautifully presented dishes. An experience, not just a meal.
💡 Tip: The Chureito Pagoda steps may be icy — wear grip shoes and take it slowly for your parents. If the climb is too much, the lower viewpoint still offers a beautiful photo.
04 Dec 23

Kawaguchiko to Hakone — Lakes & Volcanic Valleys

♨️ Hakone 🚗 Driving
▼
🌅 Morning

Sunrise at Lake Kawaguchi & Iyashi no Sato

Wake early for a stunning sunrise over Mt Fuji from your ryokan. After breakfast, drive to Iyashi no Sato — a reconstructed traditional thatched-roof village with Mt Fuji as backdrop. Peaceful, photogenic, and easy walking for all ages.

🍳 Breakfast: Traditional Japanese breakfast at ryokan — Grilled fish, miso soup, rice, pickled vegetables, tamago (egg). A wholesome and energizing start.
🌤️ Afternoon

Drive to Hakone (~1.5h) & Owakudani

Scenic drive through mountain roads to Hakone. Visit Owakudani volcanic valley (if accessible — check conditions). Try the famous black eggs (kuro tamago) boiled in sulfur springs — legend says each adds 7 years to your life!

🍜 Lunch: Amazake Chaya (Hakone Old Road) — A 400-year-old teahouse serving sweet amazake (fermented rice drink) and mochi with kinako. A magical, atmospheric pit stop.
🌆 Evening

Hakone Onsen Ryokan

Check into your Hakone ryokan. Enjoy the private rotenburo (outdoor hot spring bath) surrounded by mountain scenery. Many ryokans in the Gora or Yumoto areas offer stunning winter atmospheres.

🍱 Dinner: Kaiseki at ryokan — Another exquisite multi-course dinner, this time likely featuring Hakone specialties like tofu dishes and mountain vegetables.
💡 Tip: Owakudani Ropeway may close in bad weather or volcanic activity. Check the Hakone Navi website the morning of your visit. Have a backup plan to visit the Hakone Open-Air Museum instead.
05 Dec 24

Hakone Loop & Drive to Nagoya — Christmas Eve

⛩️ Hakone 🚗 Driving 🎄 Christmas Eve
▼
🌅 Morning

Lake Ashi Pirate Ship & Hakone Shrine

Take the scenic pirate ship cruise across Lake Ashi (20 min) — on clear days, Mt Fuji appears across the lake. Walk to the iconic Hakone Shrine torii gate standing in the water — one of Japan's most photographed spots.

🍳 Breakfast: Ryokan breakfast — Savor another traditional Japanese breakfast before checking out.
🌤️ Afternoon

Drive to Nagoya (~3h via Tomei Expressway)

Return the rental car in Nagoya or drop at a motorway-adjacent location. The drive along the Tomei Expressway passes through beautiful countryside with views of Suruga Bay and the Pacific coast. Stop at a service area (SA) along the way for regional snacks.

🍜 Lunch: Expressway Service Area — Japanese SAs are famous for being excellent! Try regional specialties like Shizuoka green tea ice cream or eel rice at a SA restaurant.
🌆 Evening

Christmas Eve in Nagoya 🎄

Arrive in Nagoya, drop off the car, and check into your hotel. Japan celebrates Christmas Eve as a romantic, festive occasion. Enjoy the Nagoya Station Christmas Illuminations — the massive light displays here are spectacular.

🍱 Dinner: Yabaton (Nagoya) — Famous for miso katsu (pork cutlet with rich Nagoya-style miso sauce). A hearty, festive Christmas Eve dinner. The Yaesu branch near the station is convenient.
💡 Tip: In Japan, Christmas Eve dinner is a big deal! KFC is a famous tradition, but we've upgraded you to miso katsu 😄. Also, Japanese Christmas cakes (shortcake with strawberries) are sold everywhere — grab one for dessert!
06 Dec 25

Nagoya to Kyoto — Christmas Day Arrival

⛩️ Kyoto 🧘 Relaxed 🎄 Christmas
▼
🌅 Morning

Nagoya Castle & Morning Stroll

Visit Nagoya Castle and the beautifully reconstructed Honmaru Palace. The golden shachihoko (tiger-fish) on the rooftop is iconic. The castle grounds are peaceful in the morning.

🍳 Breakfast: Komeda's Coffee (Nagoya original) — Order any coffee and get a free morning set: thick toast with red bean paste (ogura toast), a Nagoya specialty!
🌤️ Afternoon

Shinkansen to Kyoto (35 min)

Take the bullet train from Nagoya to Kyoto (~35 min). Check into your hotel near Kyoto Station or in the Gion area. Spend the afternoon at Nishiki Market ("Kyoto's Kitchen") — taste pickles, matcha sweets, fresh seafood skewers, and dashi.

🍜 Lunch: Nishiki Market grazing — Walk and eat! Try tamagoyaki (sweet egg), tako tamago (baby octopus stuffed with quail egg), matcha warabi mochi, and fresh yuba (tofu skin).
🌆 Evening

Gion District Evening Walk

Wander through the atmospheric Gion district — Kyoto's famous geisha quarter. Walk along Hanamikoji Street and Shirakawa Canal with its weeping willows and stone-paved lanes. You might spot a geiko (geisha) heading to an evening engagement.

🍱 Dinner: Gion Owatari — An intimate kaiseki restaurant in the heart of Gion. Seasonal multi-course dinner with impeccable presentation. Reserve well ahead for Christmas week.
💡 Tip: Christmas is not a national holiday in Japan, so shops and temples operate normally. It's actually a great day to visit since it's less crowded than the following New Year period!
07 Dec 26

Kyoto — Eastern Temples & Bamboo Grove

⛩️ Kyoto ⚖️ Balanced
▼
🌅 Morning

Fushimi Inari Taisha ⛩️

Arrive by 8:00 AM to beat the crowds at Kyoto's most iconic shrine. Walk through the mesmerizing thousands of vermillion torii gates winding up the mountainside. You don't need to climb all the way — the first viewpoint (30 min up) is perfect. The trail is gentle but paced — ideal for parents.

🍳 Breakfast: Vermillion Café (right near Fushimi Inari station) — Excellent coffee and Western-style breakfasts in a cozy space. Great egg sandwiches.
🌤️ Afternoon

Kiyomizu-dera Temple & Higashiyama

Visit the spectacular Kiyomizu-dera — the wooden terrace juts out over the hillside with panoramic views of Kyoto. Walk down through Ninenzaka & Sannenzaka — charming stone-paved lanes lined with traditional shops and teahouses.

🍜 Lunch: Omen Kodaiji (Higashiyama) — Handmade udon noodles served with seasonal vegetables and a warm dipping broth. A Kyoto institution.
🌆 Evening

Arashiyama Bamboo Grove (Sunset)

Take a bus or train to Arashiyama. Walk through the ethereal Bamboo Grove — less crowded in late afternoon. Cross the iconic Togetsukyo Bridge. If running in December, the Arashiyama Hanatouro illumination event lights up the bamboo and riverside pathways beautifully.

🍱 Dinner: Shoraian (Arashiyama) — A riverside tofu kaiseki restaurant set on a small island in the Oi River. Beautiful setting, warming winter dishes, and a tranquil end to the day. Reserve ahead.
💡 Tip: Kiyomizu-dera is on a steep hill — take the taxi up and walk down through the charming lanes instead. Much easier on knees and just as scenic!
08 Dec 27

Kyoto — Golden Pavilion, Tea & Zen Gardens

⛩️ Kyoto 🧘 Relaxed
▼
🌅 Morning

Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion)

The gold-leaf-covered pavilion reflected in the mirror pond is one of Japan's most stunning sights. Arrive early (~9:00 AM) for the best light and fewest crowds. On cold mornings, mist rising from the pond creates a dreamlike atmosphere.

🍳 Breakfast: Inoda Coffee (Sanjo Main Branch) — A beloved Kyoto institution since 1947. Their "Kyoto Breakfast" set with ham, egg, croissant, and fruit is a local morning ritual.
🌤️ Afternoon

Ryoan-ji Zen Garden & Tea Ceremony

Walk 15 minutes to Ryoan-ji Temple for the world's most famous zen rock garden — 15 stones arranged so you can never see all of them at once. Then enjoy a private tea ceremony experience at a nearby tea house (book via your hotel or Camellia Tea Experience in Gion).

🍜 Lunch: Tousuiro (Kyoto) — Specializes in Kyoto's famous yudofu (simmered tofu) — simple, elegant, and warming. A true Kyoto winter classic.
🌆 Evening

Free Evening — Rest or Explore

A relaxed evening for your parents to rest. Options: soak in your hotel's bath, browse Kyoto Station underground shopping, or take a gentle walk along the Kamogawa River promenade watching the city lights.

🍱 Dinner: Nishiki Warai (near Nishiki Market) — Casual and fun Osaka-style okonomiyaki and yakisoba. Great for a relaxed family dinner without needing a reservation.
💡 Tip: The tea ceremony is a beautiful experience for all ages and involves minimal physical activity — just sitting, sipping, and appreciating the art. Many offer English explanations. Budget ~¥3,000–5,000 per person.
09 Dec 28

Day Trip to Nara — Deer Park & Ancient Temples

🦌 Nara ⚖️ Balanced
▼
🌅 Morning

Train to Nara (45 min) & Nara Park

Take the JR Nara Line from Kyoto (~45 min). Walk to Nara Park where 1,200+ friendly deer roam freely. Buy shika senbei (deer crackers, ¥200) and feed them — they'll bow to you! Your parents will love this gentle, interactive experience.

🍳 Breakfast: Hotel breakfast in Kyoto before departure, or grab pastries at Sizuya (Kyoto Station) — famous for their karashi (mustard) bread rolls.
🌤️ Afternoon

Todai-ji Temple & Kasuga Taisha

Visit Todai-ji — home to the 15-meter bronze Great Buddha (Daibutsu), housed in the world's largest wooden building. Then walk through the atmospheric Kasuga Taisha shrine, famous for its thousands of stone and bronze lanterns.

🍜 Lunch: Kakinoha Sushi Tanaka (Nara) — Try kakinoha-zushi, Nara's specialty sushi wrapped in persimmon leaves. Delicate, flavorful, and uniquely local.
🌆 Evening

Return to Kyoto → Transfer to Osaka

Take the train back and transfer to Osaka (30 min from Kyoto). Check into your Osaka hotel in Namba or Shinsaibashi area — the heart of Osaka's food and nightlife scene.

🍱 Dinner: Dotonbori street food — Your first night in Osaka! Dive into the neon-lit madness of Dotonbori. Must-try: takoyaki (octopus balls) from Kukuru, gyoza from Chao Chao, and kushikatsu (deep-fried skewers) from Daruma.
💡 Tip: The deer in Nara are wild but accustomed to tourists. They can be slightly pushy when they see crackers — hold them up high and bow before feeding. Keep bags zipped as they'll investigate paper and plastic.
10 Dec 29

Osaka — Castle, Street Food & Shinsekai

🏙️ Osaka ⚖️ Balanced
▼
🌅 Morning

Osaka Castle & Park

Visit Osaka Castle, surrounded by moats and a large park. Take the elevator to the 8th floor observation deck for panoramic city views. The park's winter plum blossoms may be starting. Easy, flat walking around the grounds — great for parents.

🍳 Breakfast: Café Rhinebeck (Shinsaibashi) — Known for thick, fluffy pancakes and specialty coffee. A relaxed start to the day.
🌤️ Afternoon

Shinsekai & Kuromon Market

Explore the retro Shinsekai district — Osaka's old entertainment quarter with its iconic Tsutenkaku Tower and colorful signage. Then visit Kuromon Market ("Osaka's Kitchen") for the freshest sashimi, grilled wagyu skewers, and seasonal fruits.

🍜 Lunch: Kushikatsu Daruma (Shinsekai, original branch) — The iconic kushikatsu experience. Golden rule: never double-dip in the communal sauce! Try asparagus, shrimp, lotus root, and cheese skewers.
🌆 Evening

Dotonbori Night Walk & HEP FIVE

Return to Dotonbori for the full nighttime experience — the Glico Running Man sign, the moving crab sign at Kani Doraku, and the electric atmosphere. If you want a unique view, ride the HEP FIVE Ferris Wheel in Umeda — some cabins have heated kotatsu tables!

🍱 Dinner: Kani Doraku (Dotonbori main branch) — The famous crab restaurant under the giant mechanical crab sign. Order the crab kaiseki course for a luxurious multi-course crab feast. Definitely reserve in advance.
💡 Tip: Dec 29 marks the start of the New Year holiday period. Some smaller shops may begin closing early. Major attractions and restaurants stay open, but double-check any specific spots you want to visit.
11 Dec 30

Kobe Day Trip — Harbor, Beef & Chinatown

🌃 Kobe 🧘 Relaxed
▼
🌅 Morning

Train to Kobe (30 min) & Kitano-cho

Take the JR train from Osaka to Kobe (~30 min). Start with Kitano-cho — a hillside neighborhood of beautifully preserved Western-style mansions (ijinkan) from the Meiji era. The area has a charming European atmosphere and easy walking paths.

🍳 Breakfast: Nishimura's Coffee (Kobe, est. 1948) — A Kobe institution. Try their "Royal Mountain" blend with a morning toast set in an elegant, old-world interior.
🌤️ Afternoon

Nankinmachi (Kobe Chinatown) & Harborland

Walk down to Nankinmachi (Kobe's Chinatown) for street snacks — pork buns, sesame dumplings, and custard tarts. Then head to Kobe Harborland / Meriken Park for harbor views, the BE KOBE monument, and the Kobe Port Tower.

🍜 Lunch: Steakland Kobe (Sannomiya) — Authentic A5 Kobe beef teppanyaki at reasonable prices. Watch the chef grill your steak to perfection right in front of you. A must-do in Kobe!
🌆 Evening

Kobe Luminarie (if running) & Return

If the Kobe Luminarie winter illumination is still running (check dates — it usually runs in early-mid Dec), it's one of Japan's most spectacular light festivals. Otherwise, enjoy the Harborland night views before returning to Osaka.

🍱 Dinner: Mizuno (Osaka, Dotonbori) — Back in Osaka for the best okonomiyaki in the city. The yama-imo (mountain yam) batter creates an incredibly fluffy, rich pancake. Worth the wait!
💡 Tip: Kobe beef is regulated — look for the official Kobe Beef certification mark (菊の紋) at restaurants to ensure authenticity. Steakland is certified and offers excellent value compared to high-end steakhouses.
12 Dec 31

Osaka — Final Day & New Year's Eve 🎊

🏙️ Osaka 🧘 Relaxed 🎊 NYE
▼
🌅 Morning

Sumiyoshi Taisha Shrine

Visit one of Japan's oldest and most important Shinto shrines — Sumiyoshi Taisha. The unique architecture predates Chinese-influenced temple styles. As the shrine prepares for hatsumode (New Year's first visit), you'll feel the special atmosphere. The iconic arched bridge is beautiful.

🍳 Breakfast: Gram (Shinsaibashi) — Famous for their fluffy soufflé pancakes served at limited times (11:00, 15:00, 18:00). Get there early! Alternatively, enjoy a leisurely hotel breakfast.
🌤️ Afternoon

Last-Minute Shopping & Relaxation

Spend your final afternoon browsing Shinsaibashi-suji shopping arcade for souvenirs — matcha Kit-Kats, rice crackers, skincare, and Japanese crafts. Or just relax at a café and soak in the atmosphere of your last day.

🍜 Lunch: Rikuro Ojisan (Namba) — Pick up their famous jiggly cheesecake (¥965), then have a proper lunch at Ichiran Ramen (Dotonbori) — individual booths for a focused ramen experience. Perfect for a cold December day.
🌆 Evening

New Year's Eve — Toshikoshi Soba & Temple Bells 🎊

Experience a Japanese New Year's Eve! The tradition is to eat toshikoshi soba (year-crossing buckwheat noodles) for longevity. At midnight, temples across Japan ring their bells 108 times (joya no kane) — representing the 108 earthly desires in Buddhism. Head to Shitennoji Temple or return to the bustling Dotonbori area for the countdown atmosphere.

🍱 Dinner: Toshikoshi soba — Join the locals! Visit any well-regarded soba restaurant, or try Shinsaibashi Matsuya for handmade soba. Follow with a warm oshiruko (red bean soup with mochi) from a street stall near the temples.
💡 Tip: If departing on Jan 1, note that Kansai International Airport (KIX) operates normally but trains may run on a holiday schedule. Check your transport timing the night before. Consider packing the evening before to enjoy the NYE celebrations stress-free!

Packing Essentials

A winter-ready checklist for December in Japan.

👕 Clothing

  • Warm down jacket / puffer coat
  • Thermal base layers (Uniqlo HEATTECH recommended — buy in Japan!)
  • Comfortable walking shoes (waterproof preferred)
  • Scarf, gloves, warm hat
  • Layers: sweaters, long-sleeve shirts
  • Slip-on shoes (easy for temples)

📱 Tech & Travel

  • Pocket Wi-Fi or eSIM (order before departure)
  • Power bank & USB-C cables
  • Universal adapter (Japan uses Type A, 100V)
  • International Driving Permit (IDP)
  • Passport + copies
  • Travel insurance documents

🎒 Handy Items

  • Small towel (for onsen visits)
  • Foldable tote bag (for shopping)
  • Cash in yen (¥50,000+ for the family)
  • Hand warmers (kairo — also sold everywhere in Japan)
  • Basic medications & first aid
  • Grip shoe attachments (for icy Fuji-area walks)
